# Experiment 102 — recursive collision chain

## Question

Experiments 100–101 established that localized organizations can collide to form new localized products and that those products themselves retain metastable organization-class persistence.

Can a collision-generated organization participate in another organization-changing collision, or is novelty limited to one generation?

## Generational notation

This experiment uses observer-side labels only for bookkeeping:

- `G0`: localized microcycles generated by isolated two-window seeds;
- `G1`: localized products from the separation-3 collision of two identical G0 organizations;
- `G2`: localized products obtained by colliding a G1 organization with its original G0 parent;
- `G3`: localized products obtained by another collision with that same G0 parent.

No generation label exists in the substrate.

A product is counted as novel only when its canonical localized microcycle is absent from **all earlier generation catalogs**.

## Setup

Use the width-4 finite-speed local kernel and the 60-window quiescent ring.

For every collision stage choose a phase-0 frame of the existing localized organization with maximal active support. The added G0 parent is positioned at a phase-compatible location (translation by a multiple of three windows) so its isolated dynamics remain equivalent to the original parent.

Cases with initial support overlap are excluded rather than called collisions.

For accepted cases, interaction is verified causally by comparing the joint trajectory against the union of the separately evolved trajectories. A case is only advanced when those trajectories actually diverge or their independently evolved supports meet.

## Result

The aggregate is exactly the same for left-rotation and right-rotation kernels.

### G0 -> G1

From Experiment 100:

- isolated G0 localized catalog: **126 distinct microcycles** from 130 localized seeds;
- novel G1 localized products: **102 distinct microcycles**.

### G1 + G0 parent -> G2

At the clean adjacent placement used here:

- 32/102 candidate G1 states overlap the added parent at initialization and are excluded;
- remaining clean cases: **70**;
- interacting cases producing localized outcome: **70 / 70**;
- novel G2 microcycles outside G0 and G1 catalogs: **70 / 70**;
- distinct G2 keys: **70**.

### G2 + same G0 parent -> G3

All 70 cases interact.

Outcomes:

- localized active product: **38 / 70**;
- expansive active cycle: **32 / 70**.

All 38 localized outcomes are absent from the accumulated G0, G1, and G2 catalogs.

Thus:

- novel G3 microcycles: **38**;
- distinct G3 keys: **38**.

### One more collision

The 38 G3 localized products are collided once more with the same G0 parent from the opposite adjacent side.

Result:

- expansive active cycle: **38 / 38**;
- localized G4 product: **0 / 38**.

So this particular deterministic collision chain does **not** continue indefinitely as bounded localized organizations.

## Interpretation

The narrow positive result is:

> collision-generated localized organizations can themselves act as causal participants in later collisions, producing further persistent organizations whose microcycles are absent from all earlier tested catalogs.

This means Experiment 100 was not only a one-step lookup from parent pair to collision residue. The products remain dynamically usable material for subsequent organization-changing interactions.

The equally important negative result is that the tested chain saturates:

> after several bounded transformations, the next collision pushes every surviving G3 organization into an expansive cycle instead of another bounded localized product.

Therefore this is not evidence of unbounded novelty or open-ended evolution.

## Why this is stronger than the earlier period ecology

No period-2, period-3, species label, reproduction operator, mutation operator, object boundary, or collision table is supplied.

The only hard substrate is the local mutual-modulation law plus the three-phase finite-speed update geometry. `G0/G1/G2/G3` are retrospective observer labels over naturally arising localized cycles.

## Next question

The next useful question is not whether the chain can be made longer by parameter tuning.

Instead ask why some collisions remain bounded while later ones expand.

A useful audit is to compare G1, G2, and G3 organizations by:

- occupied-support size;
- cycle period;
- internal one-bit damage persistence;
- boundary-damage persistence;
- causal influence radius.

If bounded generations approach a measurable organizational capacity or interaction-density boundary before expansion, that may reveal a substrate-level limit on how much internally coupled organization can remain metastable in one local region.
