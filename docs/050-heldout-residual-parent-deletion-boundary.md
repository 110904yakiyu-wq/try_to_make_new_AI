# 050 — Sensitivity of fivefold heldout spread residual to deleting parent clusters

Experiment150 deterministically analyzes the Experiment149 113 eligible parent G0/control contrasts, using five predeclared train-probe raw partitions, three **preoutcome** control selection rules, and ever10/ever60 heldout spread event rates. The physical kernel and stored input-output records are unchanged.

For each parent, the largest possible eventual G0−control event difference under right censoring is `G0_yes + G0_unknown − control_yes`. For any subset of k matched parents removed post hoc, the maximum possible remaining upper difference is obtained by removing the k smallest parent upper differences. This is an exact adversarial deletion envelope; it must never be interpreted as a deployable parent selection policy or a formal sample confidence interval.

Across **all five fold-specific heldout panels**, the negative sign is guaranteed for arbitrary deletion of up to 37 parents (ever10) /40 (ever60) under the original first-eligible comparator; up to 25/24 under raw-bit nearest; and up to **9/9** under train t1,t3,t6 response nearest. For response-matched controls, at least 10 deleted parents are necessary for some fold to permit a nonnegative remaining sum. The exact first-nonnegative numbers by fold are 14,11,18,14,10 (ever10) and 15,12,19,15,10 (ever60). Every folded panel shares the same physical 113 parents; these are repeated measures rather than separate samples.

This indicates that physical response conditioning substantially weakens, but does not entirely erase, the observed negative spread association in the frozen eligible parent population. Neither sign identification for all 130 G0 parents nor a new organization-specific, learning, motivational or biological mechanism follows. Experiment142's broader finite arbitrary eligible-control-choice envelope includes zero; do not replace it with a more favorable conditional result.
